A practical workflow for thrift and vintage resale.

A weekend haul can leave you with a pile of unrelated jobs: identify, inspect, photograph, write, store, and eventually ship. Give each item a clear next step so the work fits the time you have.

  • Give each find an identity at intake
  • Separate inspection from research
  • Batch the setup, inspect each item
  • Draft from your notes, then check the listing
  • Make the next action visible
  • Close the record after the sale

Give each find an identity at intake

Start a record when the item comes home. A simple code such as V-014 can connect the receipt, notes, photos, and storage location. Record what you paid, where the item came from, and anything the seller told you. Treat a seller's story as a lead to investigate, not established provenance.

For an estate-sale lot, list the individual pieces before the bags and boxes get separated. Note missing parts or obvious damage now. You do not need a complex inventory system to begin; you need a record you can find again.

Separate inspection from research

Inspect the item and record visible facts first. Then make a short list of unanswered questions: maker, material, dimensions, condition, or function. A collector may know the terminology already; a thrift flipper with a mixed haul may need to work through each category separately.

When researching a maker or age, save the reference and what it actually establishes. A similar-looking object is a clue, not proof that your item is identical. Put unresolved items in a research queue instead of filling their descriptions with guesses.

Batch the setup, inspect each item

Choose a small group you can finish with the time available. Set up the photo area once, gather a ruler, and bring the items' records with you. Photograph the identifying marks and flaws while the objects are in hand, then return each one to its recorded location.

For an illustrative three-item batch, a jacket may need flat measurements, a vase may need a base-mark photo, and a board game may need a parts count. They share a photo session, but not the same inspection checklist. Keep their photos grouped by item code to prevent details from crossing between listings.

Draft from your notes, then check the listing

Use the item record to write a clear title, description, and condition notes. Read the draft beside the photos and the object. Remove unsupported claims, fill in missing measurements, and make sure the included pieces are explicit.

Choose your marketplace and complete its current category and delivery fields. Sellers may use eBay, Etsy, or Facebook Marketplace, but this workflow does not imply that Swiftora connects to those services. Handle publishing in the marketplace you use, and keep a reference to the live listing in your record.

Make the next action visible

Use a short set of statuses that describes actual work. For example: needs inspection, needs research, ready for photos, ready to list, listed, and sold. Assign one next action to an item that has stalled, such as 'measure sleeve length' rather than 'finish jacket'.

Review the queue before starting another batch. If everything is waiting for photographs, more drafting will not clear that bottleneck. Pick a manageable session around the work that is holding items up, and leave enough space to keep listed stock findable.

Close the record after the sale

Confirm the correct item and included pieces before packing. Record the actual sale amount, fees, packaging, and postage, then update the item after any later refund or return. Compare the actual costs with the estimate so the next sourcing decision starts with better information.
